What must a website's legal notice contain? Germany (§ 5 DDG) and Luxembourg

Agents now build and publish websites for businesses. A business website in Germany or Luxembourg needs provider information that visitors can find at once. In Germany this page is usually called the Impressum. Both countries list the items in one short provision.

Germany: § 5 DDG

Under § 5 (1) DDG, providers of commercial digital services, usually offered for payment, must keep the following information easily recognisable, directly reachable and permanently available:

  • name and address of establishment; for legal persons also the legal form and the authorised representative;
  • details for fast electronic contact and direct communication, including the email address;
  • the supervisory authority, if the activity needs an official licence;
  • the commercial register or similar register and the register number, if registered;
  • for regulated professions: the chamber, the professional title and the state that awarded it, and the professional rules and how to access them;
  • the VAT identification number or the business identification number, if the provider has one;
  • for an AG, KGaA or GmbH in liquidation, a statement of that fact.

The provision has further items for stated capital and for audiovisual media services. § 5 (2) says that wider information duties under other laws remain in force.

Luxembourg: Article 5 of the e-commerce law of 14 August 2000

Under Article 5 (1), a provider of information society services must give recipients and the authorities easy, direct and permanent access to:

  • its name;
  • the geographic address where it is established;
  • contact details for rapid, direct and effective communication, including the email address;
  • where applicable: professional title and professional body, the trade register number, the VAT identification number, and the authorisation to carry out the activity together with the authority that granted it.

Article 5 (2) adds a rule on prices: if the service mentions prices, they must be precise and unambiguous, and it must say whether all taxes and additional costs are included.

This summary uses the law as first published in the official journal. Later amendments were not checked.

What an agent should do

  • Ask the business for each item instead of guessing. A register number or VAT number that the agent invents is worse than a missing one.
  • Link the legal notice from every page, for example in the footer.
  • Leave out items that do not apply, such as the supervisory authority for an activity that needs no licence.
  • Have the responsible human confirm the page before it goes live.
